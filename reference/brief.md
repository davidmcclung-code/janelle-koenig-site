Janelle Koenig Website Brief

Purpose

Create a single-page, responsive personal site for Australian comedian, writer and event MC Janelle Koenig. It must sell live shows first, make the work easy to watch, and give corporate/event organisers a clear, credible path to enquire.

The approved desktop and mobile homepage mock-ups are the final visual and structural reference. This brief explains the content, behaviour and implementation intent behind them. If it conflicts with an approved mock-up, the mock-up wins.

Brand and tone

Janelle is funny, smart, approachable and professionally bookable. The site should feel editorial, warm and confident, with dry humour rather than comedy-club clichés or novelty design.

Clean cream and off-white ground

Bold black display typography, lively red accent and handwritten red script for personality moments

Generous whitespace, crisp rules and restrained use of red

Photography carries the personality. Do not create generic AI comedy imagery, fake audiences, fake client logos or invented testimonials.

Commercial priorities

Encourage visitors to see Janelle live and buy tickets.

Make video work immediately accessible.

Convert organisers into MC and event enquiries.

Send readers to Janelle's Substack.

Navigation and destinations

Single page with anchored sections. Desktop navigation is:

Shows

Watch

About

MC & Events

Primary header CTA: See Janelle Live

On mobile, use the compact logo and a clear hamburger menu. Links and controls need 44 px minimum touch targets.

Homepage structure

1. Header

Use one Janelle Koenig logo at top left, navigation centred on desktop and the red See Janelle Live CTA at top right. Keep it light and visually integrated with the hero. Do not repeat the full logo in the hero.

1. Hero

The hero is the key first impression: a very large, expressive portrait of Janelle in the denim dress on the right, with the copy column on the left. On mobile, preserve that relationship by placing the portrait beside and behind the content area without hiding the message.

Exact copy:

Comedy.

Motherhood.

Stand-up comedian, writer and indoors enthusiast.
See Janelle live around Australia.

Actions:

Watch Janelle: secondary outlined CTA with play icon, scrolls to the watch section or opens the confirmed showreel.

Keep the hero clear of logos, event listings, testimonials and social links.

Responsive behaviour

Design from the approved desktop and mobile mock-ups, testing from 320 px to 1440 px wide.

Desktop hero is a wide two-column composition. Mobile retains the large portrait and large headline without letting the portrait obscure copy or CTAs.

CTA buttons are stacked and full-width on mobile, with no narrow side-by-side controls.

Shows become a single readable list, followed by the showreel card.

Media logos may reduce to the approved mobile selection rather than becoming tiny or horizontally overflowing.

Both promotional banners stay image-led and compact on mobile.

Maintain at least 16 px body text, 44 px touch targets and zero horizontal overflow.

Content and technical rules

Keep show data separate from layout code so it can be updated quickly.

Ticket, video, Substack, social and enquiry links must be real before launch. Do not imply an action has occurred when no working integration exists.

Use genuine approved photography, with deliberate desktop and mobile crops. Existing placeholders may remain until real photography is supplied, then tune object-position rather than redesigning the layout.

Include accessible alt text, visible keyboard focus, semantic headings and labelled external links.

Optimise images for the web, preferring AVIF/WebP delivery with suitable fallbacks.

No auto-rotating carousels, fake proof points or thin placeholder pages.
